Why it matters

Background

Four Centuries

World political and economic events from 1600 to 2026, placed on a true linear year axis. Nothing is evenly spaced: crowded years look crowded, and the seventeenth century really is that empty next to the twentieth.

How to read it

  • Horizontal is time, to scale. Vertical is only stacking — when several events fall in the same span, they pile upward. Taller stacks mean busier years.
  • Colour is the category. The fourth category, Shocks, is drawn hollow rather than in a fourth hue, because no fourth hue stays distinguishable under red–green colour blindness.
  • Mark size is importance: landmark, major, notable. Landmarks sit closest to the axis.
  • Bands behind the grid are eras. The dashed line is today.
  • The ruler changes unit as you zoom: centuries across the whole board, then half-centuries, decades and single years.

About the data

Each event carries two layers. The selection, dates, categories and the one-line "why it matters" are written for this board, weighted toward worldwide political and economic history rather than any one country. The longer background and the images come from the matching Wikipedia article, checked by hand against the event — automated matching sent "Whatever it takes" to a pop song and "Nikkei peaks" to a mountaineering article before the corrections went in.

Dates carry only the precision that is well established: some events are placed to the day, some to the month, some only to the year. Search covers the whole background text, so a word like oil or famine finds every event whose article mentions it.

Coverage runs to early 2025 and thins after that; 2026 is empty. Judgements about what counts as important, and how events are categorised, are debatable by design — edit data/events.js and rebuild to make the board your own.
